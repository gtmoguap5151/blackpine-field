### Solar Generator vs. Gas Inverter: The Ultimate Off-Grid Power Setup for Truck Camping

"Solar generator" is a marketing term for a battery box with an inverter and a
charge controller. It generates nothing by itself. A gas inverter generator
actually generates — it burns fuel and makes electricity on demand. That
single difference drives every trade-off below. Here they are, in numbers.

#### Capacity vs. fuel: the real comparison

A power station's capacity is printed on the box: watt-hours. The EcoFlow
DELTA 2 stores 1,024Wh; Jackery's Explorer 1000-class units run roughly
1,000–1,070Wh depending on generation; Goal Zero's Yeti 1500X stores 1,516Wh.
Spend it and it's gone until you recharge — from the truck while driving,
from shore power, or from solar panels that, in real conditions, return a
few hundred watts for a few good hours.

A gas inverter's "capacity" is the fuel you carry. Honda's EU2200i — the
benchmark small inverter — holds 0.95 gallons and runs 3.2 hours at its
rated 1,800W load or 8.1 hours at quarter load. A five-gallon can in the
truck bed multiplies that by days. There is no battery at any price that
matches gasoline's energy per pound. There is also no gasoline that runs
silently inside a camper shell.

#### Output: closer than you'd expect

Modern 1kWh-class battery stations are not toys. The DELTA 2 outputs 1,800W
continuous (2,700W surge) — the same continuous figure as the EU2200i's
1,800W rated / 2,200W peak. The Yeti 1500X outputs 2,000W continuous. Either
side will run a microwave briefly, power tools, a fridge, lights, and every
device you own. The difference isn't what they can start — it's how long
they can keep it up.

Runtime math for the load truck campers actually run — a 12V fridge at
~45W average: a 1,024Wh station lasts roughly 18–21 hours accounting for
conversion losses. The Honda at light load sips fuel for 8+ hours per
gallon. A weekend of refrigeration is a battery problem; two weeks of it is
a fuel problem.

#### Noise, fumes, and where each can live

The battery is silent and emission-free: it can sit in the cab, the cap, or
a tent. The EU2200i is genuinely quiet *for a generator* — 48 dB(A) at
quarter load, 57 dB(A) at rated load, about conversation level — but it is
never silent, and its exhaust means it lives outside, downwind, with a CO
hazard you manage forever. In campgrounds with generator hours, the battery
works at 2 a.m. The Honda doesn't.

#### Maintenance and readiness

A lithium station (LFP models like the DELTA 2 are rated around 3,000 cycles
to 80% capacity) asks for almost nothing: keep it partly charged, cycle it
now and then. An engine asks for oil changes, fuel stabilizer or fresh gas,
and periodic runs. The generator you ignore for two years is the generator
that won't start in year three. Be honest about which owner you are.

#### Verdict by use case

- **Weekend truck camping, stealth, campground quiet hours:** battery
  station, 1kWh class. It covers lights, fridge, devices, and a CPAP for a
  night or more, in silence.
- **Long stays, high-draw tools, winter (short solar days):** gas inverter —
  or gas *plus* the battery, charging the station from the generator in one
  loud hour instead of running the engine all night. That hybrid is what
  experienced truck campers actually end up with.
- **Buy in this order:** battery first, gas second. The battery handles most
  nights; the inverter is the insurance policy for the rest.
