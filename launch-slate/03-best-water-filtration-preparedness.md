### The Best Water Filtration Systems for Long-Term Emergency Preparedness

Most filtration marketing leads with the micron rating because it's the
number that sounds most scientific. For long-term preparedness it's the
second number that decides whether your system is still running in month
three: **rated filter life**, and the labor each gallon costs you. Here are
the three architectures that matter — squeeze, gravity, and pump — judged on
flow rate, capacity, filter life, and pore size, with manufacturer figures
labeled as manufacturer figures.

A quick note on microns: 0.1–0.2 micron hollow-fiber and membrane filters
physically remove bacteria (E. coli, salmonella) and protozoa (giardia,
cryptosporidium). None of the filters in this comparison remove viruses —
in North American surface water that's a secondary concern; in a long
emergency with compromised sanitation, add a purification step (chemical
treatment or boiling) for stored water. No filter here removes dissolved
chemicals either, whatever the box art implies.

#### Squeeze: the Sawyer pattern

The Sawyer Squeeze is the benchmark: 0.1 micron absolute, up to 1.7 L/min
flow when new, ~3 oz, and a manufacturer rating of up to 100,000 gallons
with regular backflushing using the included syringe. That life rating is
one to two orders of magnitude beyond everything else on this page, at the
lowest price per gallon in the category. The trade is labor and fragility:
every liter passes through by hand pressure, flow slows as the membrane
loads (backflushing restores most of it), and the membrane must never
freeze — a frozen hollow-fiber filter can be damaged invisibly. For a
household, a Squeeze is the backup and the go-bag answer, and at its price
there's no excuse for owning one. Owning *only* one is the mistake.

#### Gravity: two very different animals

**Field gravity** — the Platypus GravityWorks 4.0L pairs two 4-liter
reservoirs with a 0.2 micron cartridge: 1.75 L/min, hands-free, 11.5 oz
total. Hang it, walk away, come back to four clean liters. Cartridge life
is ~1,500 liters — the shortest on this page — so for a fixed long-term
site it's a consumables commitment, not a one-time buy. For a camp or a
mobile group, it's the most civilized water there is.

**Countertop gravity** — the Big Berkey is a 2.25-gallon stainless system.
Its Black Berkey elements are manufacturer-rated at up to 6,000 gallons per
pair, flowing up to ~3.5 gallons per hour with two elements (up to ~7 with
four) at full head. Stated neutrally, because this brand draws strong
opinions both ways: those contaminant-reduction and life figures are the
manufacturer's published claims, supported by the independent lab reports
Berkey posts; the Black Berkey elements themselves are not NSF/ANSI
certified (Berkey's newer Phoenix elements carry NSF/ANSI 42 and 372
certification). Read the lab reports, decide what weight to give them, and
note that the math that matters — gallons per dollar at the kitchen
counter, zero power, zero pressure — is favorable either way.

#### Pump: the Katadyn pattern

The Katadyn Vario pumps ~2 L/min — the fastest per-minute figure here —
through a 0.2 micron glass-fiber element with ceramic prefilter and carbon
core, drawing from any source: a shallow creek, a rain barrel, a tank you
can't lift or hang a bag over. Cartridge life is ~2,000 liters, and pumps
have seals, handles, and moving parts that squeeze and gravity systems
don't. As a primary long-term home system it's the highest-maintenance
choice; as the tool that works in the awkward places the others can't,
nothing replaces it.

#### Verdict by use case

- **Household, fixed location, long duration:** countertop gravity as the
  primary layer. Volume with zero labor and zero power is what month three
  demands.
- **Camp / group / mobile base:** field gravity (GravityWorks class).
  Budget for cartridges.
- **Go-bag, vehicle kit, and universal backup:** Sawyer Squeeze — plus a
  second one. Its rated life means the spare may outlive the plan.
- **Awkward sources, no hang point, shallow water:** the pump earns its
  shelf space.
- **The actual recommendation:** two layers minimum — gravity for volume,
  squeeze for backup and movement. Water is the one system with no
  acceptable single point of failure.
